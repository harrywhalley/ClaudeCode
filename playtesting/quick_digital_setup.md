## How to Create These on PlayingCards.io

### Method 1: Use Their Card Creator

1. Go to https://playingcards.io/
2. Click "Create Room"
3. Click "Add Cards" → "Create Custom Card"
4. For each card type, create with:
   - **Front**: Card name + key info
   - **Back**: Generic card back (or leave blank)

### Method 2: Even Simpler - Use Standard Deck

1. Use a standard 52-card deck from PlayingCards.io
2. Create a "cheat sheet" mapping:
   - Ace of Hearts = Realism
   - King of Hearts = Positivism
   - Queen of Spades = Ethnography
   - etc.
3. Everyone references the cheat sheet

## BEST OPTION for Quick Start: Paper + Zoom

**For your very first playtest**:

1. **Print** the 30-card minimal deck (just titles and key info on paper)
2. **Cut** them out
3. **Play** in person OR
4. **Use** document camera/phone camera over Zoom to show cards
5. **Track** game state with pencil and paper

**This gets you testing in under 1 hour with zero technical hassle.**

---

## What to Focus on in First Playtest

With limited cards, test:
- ✅ Do the rules make sense?
- ✅ Can players build valid stacks?
- ✅ Is scoring clear?
- ✅ Is turn structure intuitive?
- ✅ How long does a game take?

Don't worry about:
- ❌ Perfect balance (need more cards)
- ❌ All card interactions (test subset)
- ❌ Full strategy depth (comes later)

---

## After First Test, Then Consider:

1. **If rules work**: Create more cards (expand to 60-80)
2. **If rules broken**: Fix and retest with same 30 cards
3. **If digital needed**: Consider Tabletop Simulator ($20 on Steam)
4. **If physical works**: Print full deck for serious testing

---

## My Recommendation

**Start with paper prototype**:
- Print minimal 30-card deck
- Play 2-player game in person
- Takes 1 hour total (30 min print, 30 min play)
- Zero technical barriers
- Can immediately iterate

**Then**: After confirming basic rules work, invest time in full digital or physical prototype.

**Don't**: Spend hours fighting with import formats before you know if the game even works!
