# Methodology Match: Rulebook

## 1. Overview

Methodology Match is a card game designed for arts, design, and humanities PhD researchers. Players create coherent research projects by building Research Stacks of cards. The goal is to earn the most points by aligning Ontology, Epistemology, Theory, and Methods, creating powerful methodological synergies while navigating the trials and tribulations of academic life.

## 2. Game Components

The full game consists of 153 cards:

- 5 Ontology Cards (Realism, Relativism, Constructivism, Critical Realism, Relational Ontology)
- 9 Epistemology Cards
- 22 Theory/Framework Cards
- 82 Method Cards
- 20 Object-of-Study Cards
- 15 unique Event Cards

**Players**: 2-5 players
**Play Time**: 45-60 minutes (expanded deck)

## 3. Objective

To win the game, you must have the highest total score when the game ends. Points are earned primarily by building two methodologically sound Research Stacks.

## 4. Game Setup

1. **Ontology Draft**: Separate the 5 Ontology cards from the rest. Lay them face-up where all players can see them. Starting with a randomly chosen player and proceeding clockwise, each player selects one Ontology card and places it face-down in front of them. Players may look at their own Ontology card at any time, but must keep it secret from their opponents. This card is the foundation of your first Research Stack. Return any unused Ontology cards to the box.
   - **2-3 Player Variant**: For more strategic depth, deal each player 2 random Ontology cards face-down. Each player looks at both and chooses one to keep, returning the other to the box secretly.

2. **Shared Object**: Shuffle the 20 Object-of-Study cards and place them face-down. Reveal the top card and place it in the center of the table. This is the shared research topic for all players for this game.

3. **Create the Deck**: Shuffle all remaining cards (Epistemology, Theory/Framework, Method, and Event cards) together to form the main draw pile.

4. **Deal Hands**: Deal 7 cards from the main deck to each player as their starting hand.

5. **Play Area**: Place the draw pile face-down, leaving space next to it for a discard pile.

## 5. Gameplay

Players take turns in a clockwise direction. Each turn consists of three steps: Draw, Play, and Discard.

### 5.1. Draw

Begin your turn by drawing one card. You may take either the top card from the draw pile or the top card from the discard pile.

### 5.2. Play

Perform exactly one of the following actions:

- **Start a Stack**: Play an Epistemology card face-up to begin a new Research Stack. (Your secret Ontology acts as the start of your first stack).
- **Add to a Stack**: Place a compatible Epistemology, Theory/Framework, or Method card onto one of your incomplete stacks.
- **Play an Event Card**: Reveal an Event card from your hand and resolve its effect immediately. Discard the Event card after use.

**Methodological Extremes**: When you play a Method card with a Reproducibility or Sensibility score of 2 or less, you must discard one additional card from your hand. This represents the extra effort required to work with highly specialized methods.

### 5.3. Discard

End your turn by discarding one card from your hand face-up onto the discard pile. You must always end your turn with exactly 7 cards in hand.

## 6. The Research Stack

A completed Research Stack represents a finished research project. Each of your two stacks must contain:

- Exactly 1 Ontology card
- Exactly 1 Epistemology card
- 0–2 Theory/Framework cards
- 1–3 Method cards

## 7. Scoring

Stacks are scored immediately when they are completed. Scoring is broken into three parts:

### 7.1. The Paradigm Chain Bonus

This is the main way to score points from a stack.

- **Coherent Paradigm (+10 Points)**: Awarded if your stack's Ontology is compatible with its Epistemology, AND at least one Method card has a listed synergy with that Epistemology.
- **Incompatible Paradigm (-5 Points)**: Applied if your stack's Ontology and Epistemology are explicitly listed as incompatible on the cards.
- **Mixed Paradigm (+3 Points)**: Awarded for any other completed stack that does not meet the above criteria.

### 7.2. Supplemental Scores

- **Ontology Bonus**: Each Ontology card provides a unique bonus if specific conditions are met:
  - **Realism**: +2 points if your stack includes a Method with Reproducibility 8 or higher
  - **Relativism**: +2 points if your stack includes Methods from different paradigms (e.g., one with Reproducibility 7+ and one with Sensibility 7+)
  - **Constructivism**: When you complete a stack with 2 or more Methods with Sensibility 7+, draw 1 card (then discard down to 7)
  - **Critical Realism**: +3 points if your stack includes Methods with both high Reproducibility (6+) AND high Sensibility (6+)
  - **Relational Ontology**: +3 points if your stack includes Participatory Methods, Action Research, or Ethnography

- **Theory Bonus (Tiered)**: Theory/Framework cards award points only if their alignment matches the Epistemology in the same stack. Unaligned theories are worth 0 points.
  - Highly Specialized (1 Alignment): +7 Points
  - Standard Focus (2 Alignments): +5 Points
  - Broad/Flexible (3 Alignments): +3 Points

- **Object-of-Study Affinity**: Add +2 points if your stack is thematically aligned with the shared Object-of-Study card.

- **Ethical Penalty**: Subtract -1 point for each Method card in the stack with an Ethical Risk rating of 7 or higher.

- **Cutting-Edge Bonus**: Add +3 points if the average Novelty score of all Method cards in the stack is 7 or higher.

### 7.3. Final Scoring (End of Game)

These bonuses are calculated after the game ends.

- **Objectivity Award (+5 Points)**: The player with the highest total Reproducibility score (summed from all Method cards in their completed stacks) wins this award.
- **Insight Award (+5 Points)**: The player with the highest total Sensibility score (summed from all Method cards in their completed stacks) wins this award.

(In the event of a tie for an award, all tied players receive the points).

## 8. Game End & Winning

The game ends immediately when one player completes their second Research Stack, or when the draw pile runs out.

After calculating any Final Scoring awards, the player with the highest total points is the winner.
